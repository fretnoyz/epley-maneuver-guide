A guided home reference

Epley maneuver

A calm, step-by-step guide for positional vertigo that a clinician has identified as BPPV.

Use this only if BPPV is familiar or has been diagnosed. This guide does not diagnose the cause of dizziness. The correct side and maneuver depend on which inner-ear canal is involved.

Which ear is affected?

Choose the side your clinician identified, or the side that reliably brings on your usual brief spinning with ordinary movements. If you’re unsure, don’t guess—ask a clinician or vestibular physical therapist.

Select a side to continue.

Stop and seek urgent medical care if dizziness comes with new weakness or numbness, trouble speaking or walking, double vision, fainting, chest pain, or a sudden severe headache. For new or persistent vertigo, get medical advice rather than relying on this guide.

00:45
Stay in this position for about 30–60 seconds, or until the spinning settles.

That’s the sequence. Sit quietly until steady. You may feel temporarily off balance. Have someone nearby if you feel unsteady, and don’t drive while dizzy.

About this guide and sources

These instructions are a general educational aid and may not fit every cause or type of positional vertigo. A clinician can confirm the diagnosis and affected side, and show you the maneuver safely.
